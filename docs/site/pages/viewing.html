Viewing

Everything on this page changes only how the processed sweep is shown. It applies at once, without processing again, and is remembered for the next session.

Plots and derivatives

The toolbar chooses what the plots show:

Plot
R(B)/R(0), Data, R(B)/R(B-AVR) or R(B)/R(B-ΔB) (Ctrl+1 to 4); what each one is.
Derivative
Off, 1st or 2nd (Alt+1, 2, 3), along the energy (d/dE) or along the field (d/dB).
per unit
Off: the derivative is taken per data point, as in the old versions. On: it is divided by the real step, so d/dE is per cm⁻¹, meV or THz and d/dB is per tesla. An energy derivative per unit therefore changes its values with the unit; one per data point or along the field does not.

The plot tabs are Map, Stacked and Reference; the line next to them describes the map shown, e.g. R(B)/R(0) · 2nd derivative d/dE per meV.

The energy unit

The Unit switch in the toolbar (cm⁻¹ | meV | THz) converts everything at once: the maps and their axes, the view ranges, the colour levels of per-unit derivatives, the picked points, the model parameters and curves, the journal figure window and every export. Nothing is processed again, and switching back gives exactly the numbers you had.

Inside, energies are kept in cm⁻¹, the unit of the files, with 1 meV = 8.0656 cm⁻¹ and 1 THz = 33.35641 cm⁻¹ (the factors of the earlier versions).

View ranges

The inspector › View holds the ranges of the plot on screen: Field B and Energy E, shared by the map and the stacked plot, and the Intensity of the stacked plot. Each range is Auto, which fits the data, or Fixed, with a slider and two fields; the note below it gives the range of the data. Drag a slider handle to move one end, or the bar between the handles to move the whole range at its width, as far as the slider goes (Shift+arrow keys do the same); both fix the range.

On a plot, drag to pan, scroll over the plot or an axis to zoom, and double-click to fit. Any such change fixes the range; Auto or Fit to data (A) fits the data again. Box zoom (Z) zooms to a dragged rectangle.

Colour maps and levels

The inspector › Colour chooses the colour map and its levels:

  • Colour maps: Auto (magma for maps, grey for derivatives), magma, inferno, viridis, plasma, turbo, grey and bipolar.
  • Auto levels span the 1st to 99th percentile of the whole map, not only the part zoomed in.
  • Fixed keeps the levels you set, by typing them, by dragging on the histogram or on the colour scale next to the map.
  • Symmetric centres the levels on 1 for ratios and on 0 for derivatives; it is not offered for the raw data.

The levels are remembered for each combination of plot kind, derivative, axis and per-unit setting, so going back to a view brings back its levels. To start with, ratios are fixed at 0.9 – 1.1 (0.98 – 1.02 for R(B)/R(B-ΔB)), the data map is on Auto and derivatives are symmetric about 0. The Histogram of the map values folds open below the levels.

Colour scales

Each map has its colour scale beside it, in one of two styles that a switch in the plot toolbar chooses for all maps:

  • the classic histogram scale, with the distribution of the values and draggable levels (the default);
  • a slim colour bar with two handles, which leaves more room for the map. With slim bars the inspector shows the histogram instead.

A click on the handle between a map and its scale slides the scale away or back; the eye button in the plot toolbar shows or hides all of them.

Legend

The legend button in the plot toolbar shows a legend in the map or in the stacked plot, each on its own; it starts off. It lists the picked curves with their markers, the current curve in bold, and on the map every model drawn, with its line and its branches in one row, e.g. Zeeman / magnon · 3 branches; a fit not yet applied gets a row of its own. The legend follows the points, the models and the unit as they change, and hides while there is nothing to list.

Drag the legend anywhere on the plot: it keeps that place when you zoom, pan or resize the window, and the next session remembers it. A press on the legend only moves it, whichever tool is active. For the legend of a journal figure, see Journal figures.

Stacked spectra

The Stacked tab draws the spectra above one another. The inspector › Traces section sets the offset between spectra, which spectra are shown (all, every 2nd or every 4th) and colour by field, which takes the colours from the colour map (Auto uses viridis). The intensity range follows the offset while it is on Auto. Picked points are drawn on their traces, and you can pick on this plot too.

Sliding panels and appearance

The side panel, the inspector, the log drawer and the colour scales all slide open and closed and never cover the plot. The dividers can be dragged, and the window remembers the sizes. A click on the open panel's rail button closes it; the panel button in the plot toolbar shows or hides the inspector, and the Log button in the status bar opens the log.

The button at the right end of the toolbar, or View › Appearance, switches between light, dark and the system's appearance.

Quick exports

  • Export › Data table… (Ctrl+E) saves the map shown as a tab-separated table, in the unit shown (format).
  • Export › Quick image (PNG/SVG)…, or the image button in the plot toolbar, saves the plot as it is on screen, with its colour scale.
  • Export › Add plot type to the file name appends the plot to the name of a data table, e.g. sweep_Ratio_2ndDer.csv.

For figures to print, use the journal figure window.
