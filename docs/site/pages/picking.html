Picking points

Picked points are the transition energies you read off the map: for each curve, such as a Landau-level transition, one energy per field. They are what models are fitted to, and they can be exported as a table.

The Points panel

The first Process creates a point table with a row for every field of the map. The Points panel shows it:

Pick on the map / Auto-pick
Turn on picking by hand (P) or auto-pick (W); the line below says what the two auto-pick modes do. With no points yet, the table points to both.
Curves
Each curve is a chip with its colour and number of points. Click a chip to make it the current curve, New to start another one; type in the name field to rename the current curve, and the bin button deletes it.
Points
The field and energy of every point of the current curve, in the unit shown, each with a button that removes it.
Markers on the plots
Current curve only, All curves, or Hidden.
Start a new table on the next Process
Off (the default) keeps the picked points when you process again; on starts an empty table for the next sweep.
Import… / Export…
Read or save a point table: one column per curve, one row per field (format). Import replaces the curves; undo brings them back.

Picking by hand

Turn on the Pick points tool with P, with the target button in the plot toolbar or with Pick on the map in the Points panel. Then:

  • Click the map: the current curve gets a point at the field of the map column under the click, with the energy of the click. A curve has one point per field, so a second click at the same field moves the point.
  • Alt-click (⌥-click on macOS) removes the current curve's point under the cursor, or else its point at that field.
  • Esc stops picking.

A hint above the plot names the curve you are picking, e.g. Picking LL 2 · on the map · Alt-click to remove · Esc to stop.

On the stacked plot

With the Pick tool on the Stacked tab, click a trace: the point goes to that trace's field, at the energy of the click. Alt-click a trace to remove its point. The points are drawn on the traces, so you can check a pick against the spectrum itself.

Undo and redo

Ctrl+Z undoes and Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes every change to the points: picks and removals, accepted auto-picks, imports, new, renamed and deleted curves. The Edit menu names the step it would undo.

Auto-pick

The Auto-pick tool (W, the wand in the plot toolbar, or Auto-pick in the Points panel) finds the points of a line for you. It works on the map, on whatever map is shown: plot kind, derivative and unit. A strip of options opens above the map.

Track

Click a line. Auto-pick finds the feature nearest to the click and follows the line field by field in both directions, predicting each next energy from the last three points. It bridges up to two fields where the line fades, and stops where it is lost for longer.

Detect

Choose a Shape and draw a region on the map. Every line inside it is found and drawn as a thin line. The line that continues the current curve's points is chosen, or else the longest one; it carries diamonds. Click another line to choose it instead.

Box
Drag a box. Its corners resize it.
Rotated
Drag a box, then turn it with its round handle, for example along a sloping line, so that a line next to it stays out.
Ellipse
Drag the box around the ellipse. The square handle resizes it and the round one turns it.
Polygon
Draw a loop around the lines with the mouse button held down. It becomes a polygon with a handle on each corner: a click on an edge adds a corner, a right-click on a corner removes it.

Only the lines inside the region count, not those elsewhere in the box around it. The region stays on the map: drag it to move it, or drag a handle to reshape it, and the search runs again while it changes. A drag beside the region draws a new one; a drag on the plot legend moves the legend instead. Panning still works with the middle mouse button, and zooming with the wheel.

Options

Feature
Max (peaks), Min (dips, e.g. absorption lines in R(B)/R(0)), Rising or Falling (inflection points, where the values grow or drop fastest with energy).
Window
How far from the prediction (or from the click) a feature may be, in the unit shown. Empty means automatic: 2 % of the energy range, shown in the field.
Smooth
Off, or a Savitzky–Golay filter over 5, 9 or 15 points before searching, for noisy spectra.
Prominence
How much a feature must stand out to count. Empty means automatic, from the noise of the searched spectra; the value used is shown in the field. Raise it if noise is picked, lower it if a weak line is missed.

Changing an option searches again at once, from the same click or region. The status line says what was found, e.g. 5 lines found: 31 points for LL 2 on the one with diamonds, or why nothing was found and what to change.

Accept or discard

What auto-pick finds is only a preview until you press Accept: then the points go into the current curve, replacing its points at the same fields and keeping the others, as one undo step. Discard, Esc, the other mode, another tool or another tab drop the preview and the region. A unit switch converts the preview and the region without searching again.
